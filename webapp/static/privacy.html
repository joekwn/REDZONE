REDZONE

Privacy

Last updated {{UPDATED}} · REDZONE is a student prototype built for the NASA Space Apps Challenge. This page explains what it stores and shares. It is not legal advice.

Using the map as a guest

You can use the map, search, statistics, outlook and look-ups without an account. REDZONE does not use advertising or analytics trackers.

  • Your browser stores a few settings on your own device (selected tab, side panel width, map style). They never leave your device.
  • The server sees your IP address with each request, as every website does. It is kept in memory only, to apply limits against abuse (for example, the number of FIRMS look-ups per hour), and it is forgotten when the server restarts.

You can delete your account at any time in the Account tab. This removes your account, sessions, events, priority sites and their alerts straight away. Copies may remain in server backups for up to 14 days.

Services REDZONE contacts

Some features send limited information to outside services:

  • Your location (Near me): if you allow it, your browser gives your position to the page. It is used to search nearby fires and the weather forecast and is not stored unless you save it as a priority site. If you do not allow it, the server asks ipapi.co or ipwho.is for an approximate location of your IP address.
  • Place search: what you type is sent by the server to Photon and Nominatim (OpenStreetMap).
  • Fire-risk outlook and event checks: coordinates (rounded to about 10 m) are sent to Open-Meteo for the weather forecast.
  • Fire data: map areas and dates are sent to NASA FIRMS for live updates and look-ups. No personal data is included.
  • Map tiles and libraries: your browser loads map images from CARTO, Esri or OpenStreetMap, and code libraries from unpkg and jsDelivr. These services receive your IP address under their own privacy policies.
