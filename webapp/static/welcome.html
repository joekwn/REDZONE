About

What is REDZONE?

Satellites detect active fires all over the planet several times a day, but the raw data comes in different formats from different sensors. REDZONE brings two of NASA's fire products together into one checked dataset, puts it on an interactive map, and adds tools that turn detections into answers: where fires burned, how risky the coming days look, and whether a place you care about had fires nearby.

REDZONE was built as a prototype for the NASA Space Apps Challenge. Its goal is to show how open satellite data can be made reliable and easy to use, without hiding the limits of that data.

The data

Two NASA sensors, kept honest

All fire detections come from NASA's Fire Information for Resource Management System (FIRMS). The two products see fire differently, so REDZONE keeps their original values and labels every detection with its sensor.

MODIS Collection 6.1

  • Satellites Terra and Aqua
  • Pixels of about 1 km
  • Confidence from 0 to 100
  • 3,386,208 detections

VIIRS 375 m

  • Satellite NOAA-21
  • Pixels of 375 m, so it sees smaller fires
  • Confidence: low, nominal or high
  • 17,780,994 detections

How we prepared it

Standardize

Raw files split into 87 chunks with the same 17 columns, original text values kept exactly.

Harmonize

Every file forced into one layout, with a sensor label on every detection. No values changed.

Check

No duplicates found. Coordinates, dates, times and confidence all valid.

Flag, don't delete

8 negative and 7,922 zero fire-power values, and 391 polar points, are flagged, not removed.

We also tested whether fire power can be converted between the two sensors. It cannot be done reliably (single places differ by about 3 times), so each sensor's fire power is always shown separately.

New and past days

These steps were built and checked on the one-year set. The same rules are now written once and applied automatically to every new file from the FIRMS API: new days every few hours, and past days on request. Past MODIS days use NASA's standard-quality product; past VIIRS NOAA-21 days are only available as near-real-time data, and only as far back as FIRMS keeps them. Rows that break a rule are set aside, never silently dropped.

Features

What you can do

🗺

Fire map

A heat grid when zoomed out and every detection when zoomed in, filtered by date, sensor and confidence, with live statistics.

🔎

Search and "near me"

Find any place like on a map app, or use your location to see fires detected nearby.

🌡

7-day fire-risk outlook

Weather forecasts turned into a fire weather index, adjusted with the area's fire history.

📅

Event checks account

Save an event; 12 hours before it starts, REDZONE checks for fires and fire weather nearby.

🛡

Priority sites account

Mark places to protect, such as a data center, and get alerts when new fires appear inside the radius.

🕰

Look up older data

Ask NASA FIRMS for any area and up to 10 days, back to 2000 for MODIS, cleaned with the same rules. Shown and downloadable, not saved.

You can use the map, search and outlook as a guest. A free account is needed to save events and priority sites.

Limitations

What REDZONE cannot tell you

Rules tested on one year

Harmonization and cleaning were developed and tested on 1 November 2025 to 1 November 2026. Days added outside that window use the same rules automatically but have not been audited as deeply. Fire history and statistics only cover the days that are loaded.

Satellites can miss fires

Each satellite passes a place only a few times a day, and clouds or smoke can hide fires. Some detections are not wildfires (volcanoes, industrial heat). REDZONE never says a place is "safe".

The outlook is a risk level

It combines weather with the fire history that is loaded. It does not include vegetation or drought, and it does not predict that a fire will start.

A prototype

Features, accounts and data handling are still being developed. Use REDZONE for exploration and learning, not for emergency decisions.
